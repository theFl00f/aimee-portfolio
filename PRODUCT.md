# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Creative directors, art directors, and senior designers at editorial magazines, packaging studios, in-house brand teams, and independent agencies. Freelance clients ranging from founders to small businesses. All arriving with a single question: does this designer have a point of view?

Aimee's dream roles sit in editorial and packaging — audiences who can tell the difference between a template and a sensibility.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio that gets Aimee hired. Not a resume with pictures — a designed object that demonstrates, by its own existence, that she can hold an editorial eye, commit to a concept, and make restraint feel considered rather than absent.

Success looks like: a creative director bookmarking it, forwarding it, or reaching out. Not likes. Not impressions.

## Brand Personality

Warm professional. Rigorous work, accessible person. The kind of designer who studied medieval manuscripts to build a bookstore brand and thought that was a completely natural thing to do. Culturally literate, conceptually driven, not interested in impressing other designers — interested in resonating with the right client.

Three words: **grounded, expressive, precise**.

## Anti-references

- Behance / Dribbble aesthetics — shiny, trend-chasing, likes-optimized, speaking to designers not clients
- Over-animation — scrolljacking, particle effects, performative motion that upstages the work
- Generic agency template — dark hero, big stat callouts, identical case-study grid

## Design Principles

1. **The work is the point.** Every layout choice is a stage, not a performance. If a design element makes itself more interesting than the work it frames, it's wrong.
2. **Restraint that breathes.** Minimal doesn't mean empty. White space is a decision, not a default — it creates room for the work to land.
3. **Editorial eye, not template.** Typography and layout should reflect a designer's sensibility. Aimee's projects span medieval manuscripts to fashion surrealism to corporate rebrand — the portfolio structure should feel curated, not filed.
4. **Warmth through clarity.** Approachable doesn't mean casual. The site should feel like meeting someone who is generous with their thinking, not like reading a pitch deck.
5. **Earn every animation.** Motion that reveals, grounds, or guides is good. Motion that performs or distracts is not. The bar is always: would this be better still?

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. High-contrast black-and-white base naturally supports this. Reduced-motion preference should suppress or simplify all transitions — already implemented in the typewriter hero. Color choices (when any are introduced) must meet 4.5:1 contrast on body text.
